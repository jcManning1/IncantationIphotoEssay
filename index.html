In Defense of the Poor Image

What stands out most to me, is when Steyerl proposes, " Focus is identified as a class position, a position of ease and privilege, while being out of focus lowers one's value of an image". The poor image argues that prestige and power have been smuggled into technical specs. In today's society, we have introduced and intertwined class and status with digital resolution. High resolution is not only “better looking.”, it can be the visual equivalent of a gated neighborhood/flagship store: expensive to produce, expensive to store, expensive to display, and often legally fenced.

Ask yourself, "What do I treat as “serious” in my own work simply because it is clean?" A 4K file projected in a film gallery can feel more “finished” than a phone clip that actually reached people. I used to apologize for compression/file quality of work. Now I want to know what the compression is doing. Who does it include? Who does it exclude? What kind of attention does it assume?

The title “In Defense" is not neccessarily a love letter to ugliness/degraded quality. Defending the poor image does not mean celebrating bad craft, pretending every meme is resistance, or refusing high resolution on principle. It means refusing the idea that visual degredation/poverty is automatically failure. Sometimes degredation is the price of movement. Sometimes it is the mark of abandonment. Sometimes it is a style that rich institutions later copy.

An Account of Peach Blossom Spring

The fisherman is a medium. He is a person who crosses from one world into another and comes back with an account. The villagers understand this. Their hospitality is real; their warning is technical. The moment the valley is described to the prefect, it becomes an object of administration: something to be located, verified, possibly annexed.

The path-marking is the first attempt to turn a lived passage into a reproducible route. The failure to document the route is the point. Some realities will survive the format that would make them public. This is why the piece talks artists who works with images, recordings, installations, archives, or platforms. Media is not only how we show a world, media is often how a world gets found, named, and then lost again, because naming attracts the wrong kind of attention.
